A city builder of the steppe

Produce. Trade. Grow.

In the spirit of the great city builders, set among the yurts, caravanserais and turquoise domes of Central Asia. Every road, every well and every shrine has a purpose.

Produce

Herds, fields & workshops

Graze sheep, sow wheat, quarry stone, mine iron. Turn wool into felt, clay into pottery and cocoons into silk.

Trade

Caravans of the Silk Road

Five great cities each crave different goods. Sell at your gate, or send your own camels east for a better price.

Grow

A tribe becomes an empire

Fed, watered, blessed and schooled, your people rise from felt tents to wind‑tower mansions, and the camp becomes a capital.

Nine ways to live

From felt tent to wind‑tower mansion

Homes grow only when their needs are met. Bring water, then food, then a shaman's blessing, a healer, tales and music, a school, a bathhouse and a quiet green street, and watch each house rise.

Made for your hand

A living town in your palm

Water carriers, shamans, storytellers and tax collectors walk your streets, and every home they pass is served. Children play, women carry water from the well, and smoke rises from every hearth.

  • Plan, then buildDrag out a road or a whole quarter. Nothing is built until you tap Build, so a slip of the thumb costs nothing.
  • Two minutes or two hoursYour city saves itself as you play. Pick it up on the bus or lose an evening to it.
  • A quiet screenPainted icons and a slim bar leave the town in view. Advisors and cards hold the detail.

Trials of the steppe

The Great Khan expects tribute

He asks for horses, felt or gold for his wars. Send them and rise from clan elder to his right hand. Refuse him three times and his army rides.

  • Bandits and raidsBuild garrisons, mount your guards on your own horses, and ride out to burn the bandits' camp.
  • Seasons that matterFields lie fallow in winter, droughts and floods come, and the herds fall sick in autumn.
  • Fire, plague and earthquakeFire wardens and healers walk your streets and watchmen keep thieves away. Neglect them and you'll feel it, and even a well-kept town can feel the earth shake.
